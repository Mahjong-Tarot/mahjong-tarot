# What Is Purple Star?

Purple Star is one of the two major systems of Chinese astrology, the other being Bazi. The format used today was established during the Ming Dynasty, roughly 500 to 600 years ago, so it has been around a long time. It is also known as Zi Wei Dou Shu.

It is an extremely comprehensive system. Astrologers use anywhere from 14 to 115 stars, governed by many rules and combinations. The complexity is real: a single star can carry around 1,700 different possibilities. The rules for calculating and interpreting the stars are complicated, and it takes years of study to do a Purple Star reading properly.

## Origins

The reading was originally created for the Emperor, and by extension the close members of his court. That is why the stars were named after the Emperor and his court officials, so the system spoke to them directly. A few astronomical bodies, like the sun and the moon, were included as well.

For the first couple hundred years the system was tightly controlled inside the palace. Then it leaked out, and literate common people in China began getting readings. Street astrologers added many more stars to differentiate their work from one another, which is how the system grew from about 14 stars to 115 over a couple hundred years.

## How the Chart Is Built

A Purple Star chart resembles a Western chart in structure. There are 12 palaces, similar to the houses in Western astrology, and each palace carries one of the 12 signs: rat, ox, tiger, rabbit, dragon, snake, horse, sheep, monkey, rooster, dog, and pig.

Your date and time of birth is the fundamental information that governs the construction of the whole chart. The exact hour is absolutely essential. Without the correct hour, a Purple Star reading cannot be done accurately.

## The Palaces

The most important palace is the Ming palace, or Life Palace. It influences all the other palaces and is where you see your overall fate.

Other key palaces cover:

- Wealth
- Marriage and relationships (spouse, friends, coworkers, children, bosses and supervisors)
- Siblings
- Parents
- Health
- The Traveling Palace, which addresses where you settle for work, whether you stay near where you were born or are pulled away to travel, and your chances of success when you do travel
- Property
- The Fortune and Virtue Palace, also called the Leisure Palace, which is about your character and almost your karma. It focuses on what you do when you are not obligated, whether you build virtue and do good works or simply chase your pleasures.

## Time Periods

A key feature of the chart is that it assigns each palace a decade. You can read a 10 year slice of your life or narrow it down to a single year. Beyond describing your traits and situation, the chart speaks to your luck during those periods and the luck in each aspect of your life.

## Stars and Luck

Luck depends on the kinds of stars sitting in a palace. There are four levels: highly auspicious, auspicious, inauspicious, and highly inauspicious.

There are also major and minor stars. Purple Star has 14 major stars, mostly named after the Emperor and his court, and these include the sun and the moon. The major stars mainly determine whether a palace, and the corresponding area and time of life, is auspicious or not. The minor stars add a great deal of additional information.

Sometimes a palace has no major star, which can indicate a weak area and time of life. For this there is a technique called borrowing: you go to the opposite palace, across the chart, and borrow its stars to make up for the absence.

## Why Every Reading Is Unique

Reading the chart is complex because you have to know the qualities of each star, which vary from palace to palace, how the stars relate to one another within a palace, and how each star relates to the life area that palace governs. The result is that almost no two people share the same reading. It would be highly unusual to ever meet someone with an identical chart, even among people close to you in age.

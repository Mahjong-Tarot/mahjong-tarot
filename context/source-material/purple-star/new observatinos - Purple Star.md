# Purple Star

Notes from the June 23, 2026 conversation between Bill and David Hajdu.

## Getting back into it

After a ten year hiatus, Bill is returning to Purple Star (Zi Wei Dou Shu) readings, picking up the second half of the practice: actually calculating people's fortunes. The work is slow going because there is a lot to relearn, but he is finding the system remarkable in how much it can reveal about a person's life.

## Combinations are everything

Just like mahjong card readings, combinations drive Purple Star. The difference is depth. Two stars can carry a meaning as a combination, but that meaning shifts depending on which of the 12 palaces (houses) they sit in. So it is not enough to learn a combination. You also have to learn how that combination behaves in each palace it could land in. Purple Star has far more combinations to track than the other systems Bill works with.

## The question of how many stars to use

A core technical decision in Purple Star is how many stars to include in a reading. The range runs wide:

- About 14 major stars for a basic reading
- Up to roughly 36 when you add Class B stars
- All the way to 115 when you include Class C (very minor) stars

Bill has decided his readings will be different from the standard approaches. Rather than committing to a fixed number of stars, he is building his readings around utility. If a minor star does not add anything meaningful, or just duplicates what a major star already says, he leaves it out. So far he has worked through about 10 of the Class C stars and is keeping only about half of them. The goal is a high quality, deliberately curated product rather than one based on a predetermined formula.

## Bottom line

Purple Star is an intellectually demanding system, and Bill is treating his return to it as a serious relearning and curation project, with the aim of producing a distinctive, utility-driven reading.

# Lose, Lose, Lose, Win: The Sheep's Way of Getting What It Wants

The Sheep can lose and lose and lose and lose and lose an argument. And all of a sudden, the Sheep wins.

If you read [Monday's post](/blog/posts/bright-side-sheep-year-2027-prosperity), you know that's how I expect people to get ahead in 2027. The Horse year rewarded the aggressive, Yang, macho approach. The Sheep year rewards a Yin approach, almost a Taoist one. You succeed by yielding. You come at it indirectly. You take your time.

It sounds lovely. Now try doing it on a Tuesday, with a colleague talking over you.

I'm the Firepig. In over 35 years of doing readings, I've noticed something about advice like "be patient." Everybody agrees with it, and almost nobody can tell you when it applies. From the outside, yielding and giving up look exactly the same. One of them wins in the end. The other one just loses slowly.

The Mahjong Mirror is how you tell them apart.

## First, Understand What Kind of Fire This Is

Before you yield to anything, know what you're working with.

The Horse carries Yang fire, which is fire out of control. The Sheep carries Yin fire. Controlled fire. There's still a lot of passion next year, for good and for difficulties, but it's a fire you can bank, direct and bring up slowly.

That's the whole logic of the Sheep's method. It doesn't lack heat. It chooses when to use it.

And don't confuse the Sheep's peaceful nature with a quiet year. Emotions will run strong, and there will be flare-ups when they get out of control. Yielding next year takes more self-control, not less.

## The Mirror Looks at Every Decision From Four Angles

When you're deciding whether to push or give way, walk through these in order. It takes about ten minutes and a notebook.

### Step 1: The Central Theme. What Is This Actually About?

Write down what you think the fight is about. Then ask what's really at stake.

Most arguments are about something smaller than what matters. You're arguing over who handles a project, and the real issue is whether you'll be respected on the next one. You're arguing about dinner with your in-laws, and the real issue is whose family comes first.

Once you can name the real thing, you'll often see that you can lose the small argument and keep the real one alive. That's the first secret of the Sheep. It gives up the point. It doesn't give up the goal.

**Ask yourself:** If I lost this argument today, would I actually lose what I care about?

### Step 2: Know Thyself. Who Are You in This Decision?

Next, be honest about the state you're in.

Are you calm, or are you hot? Are you yielding because it's smart, or because you're tired and afraid of the conversation? Are you pushing because it matters, or because you've spent a whole Horse year getting used to pushing?

This is the angle that separates patience from surrender. Patience knows what it wants and is willing to wait for it. Surrender has stopped wanting anything.

**Ask yourself:** If this were easy, would I still choose to wait?

### Step 3: The Opposition. What Is Really in Your Way?

Now look at what's standing against you, and be careful here. The obvious opponent is often not the real one.

Sometimes it's the other person, and they simply need time to come around on their own. In that case, more pressure only hardens them. Give them room, and you may find the argument wins itself.

Sometimes the opposition is the moment. Everyone's emotions are up, nobody's listening, and anything you say will be used against you. Wait for a better day.

And in a fire year, the opposition is very often your own temper. Your urge to have the last word can cost you more than anyone in the room ever could.

**Ask yourself:** If I stop pushing for a week, what happens to the thing in my way?

### Step 4: The Future. What Is This Decision Building Toward?

Finally, look down the road. Picture this same situation a year from now.

If you yield today, does it build something? Does it keep a relationship healthy, or leave a door open? Then yielding is an investment.

If it just teaches people that they can walk over you, or if it means quietly abandoning something you were meant to do, then it's not the Sheep's method at all. Stand your ground, gently. The Sheep is soft. It isn't absent.

**Ask yourself:** A year from now, will I be glad I gave way here?

## Where to Yield, and Where to Go Slow

Once you've got the habit, you'll find the year sorts itself into a few simple rules.

**In a conflict, seek resolution.** The Sheep is very much a peaceful sign. If you're at odds with somebody, the odds next year are in your favor, as long as you're willing to go first.

**In money, go slow.** This is where Sheep caution is a plain virtue. Take it easy. Make sure you know what you're doing. Aim for stability and conservative investments. Fire can make even a Sheep a little less careful than usual, so [hold on to your plan](/blog/posts/fire-sheep-2027-money-career-caution).

**In love, loosen up now and then.** It may be all right to throw caution to the wind every so often when it comes to romance. It's the one area where the Sheep year forgives a little impulse. [It's a great year for it](/blog/posts/fire-sheep-2027-love-passion-builds).

> The Sheep gives up the point. It doesn't give up the goal.

## The Long Way Around Is a Skill

This doesn't come naturally to most of us, especially after a year in which the Horse told us to run at everything.

You have until February 6 to practice. Pick one disagreement that's in front of you right now and take it through the four angles. You may find you've been fighting the wrong fight. You may find you should keep fighting, more quietly. Either way, you'll know.

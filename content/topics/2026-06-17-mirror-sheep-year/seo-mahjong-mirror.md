## FAQ Schema

**Q: What is the Mahjong Mirror?**
A: The Mahjong Mirror is a decision-making framework developed by Bill Hajdu, built around four angles of reflection: the central theme (what this is really about), self-knowledge (who you are in this situation), the opposition (what is actually blocking you), and the path forward (what the solution actually looks like). It uses Mahjong cards to surface what's really happening.

**Q: How do I use the Mahjong Mirror to prepare for the Year of the Sheep 2027?**
A: Apply the four angles to the transition itself. First angle: name the central theme of what you've been building in 2026. Second angle: identify who you are in transitions (galvanized or destabilized). Third angle: name what opposes a smooth transition (unfinished business, habits built for speed, loose financial patterns). Fourth angle: build emotional steadiness, tend key relationships, and create a spending plan before February 6, 2027.

**Q: Why does preparation for the Sheep year need to happen now, in the Horse year?**
A: The Fire Horse year still has Yang energy and forward momentum. Yang energy is easier to work with for making clear decisions, addressing unfinished business, and building new habits. The Sheep year's Yin energy is slower and more reflective. What you build now, while the Horse year is still active, is what you carry into the calmer year. Waiting until the Sheep year begins means building foundations under slower, more emotional conditions.

**Q: What are the biggest risks in the Fire Sheep year 2027?**
A: Financial looseness (the Sheep spends more easily than it earns), emotional reactivity (feelings run higher in a Yin fire year), and career drift (the slower pace can make it harder to stay focused on professional goals). All three are addressable with preparation during the remaining months of the Horse year.

**Q: What is the difference between the First and Fourth Angles of the Mahjong Mirror?**
A: The First Angle identifies the central theme of a situation: what it is really about at the deepest level. The Fourth Angle identifies the solution: the concrete path forward. The First Angle is understanding; the Fourth Angle is action. Both are required for a complete picture.

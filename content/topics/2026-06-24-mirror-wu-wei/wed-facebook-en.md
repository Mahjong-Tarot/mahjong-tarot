Most Tao Te Ching translations read like they need a master's degree to decode.

Lao Tzu didn't write for scholars. He wrote for common people, in images so plain that anyone would recognize them. A teapot that has to be emptied before it can be refilled. Spokes arranged around an empty hub. Wood before the carving.

These images aren't decorative. They're instructions.

The teapot: empty your assumptions before you decide. The hub: protect the open space where honest reflection happens, or the wheel stops turning. The uncarved wood: stay in the state of full possibility a little longer before you fix your direction.

That's the same discipline behind the Mahjong Mirror. Empty the cup. Read the current. Act with it instead of forcing your way through.

Most Tao translations bury this under abstraction. That's why I'm working on my own version: plain language, practical applications on the right-hand page, ideas a common person anywhere can actually use.

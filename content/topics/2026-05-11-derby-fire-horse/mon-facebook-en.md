I went to Louisville to see Fire Horse energy with my own eyes.

What I got was the most astonishing Derby in the race's 152-year history.

Golden Tempo was dead last at the three-quarter mark. Thirty seconds later, he was first. Not second. First. Done. The 23-to-1 long shot. The horse whose owner wasn't on camera talking big that morning.

Cherie DeVaux, the first woman trainer to win the Derby in 152 years.

A jockey who had lost six previous Derbies, won both Friday and Saturday with two different trainers and owners, and is now a Triple Crown jockey.

A 70-to-1 horse in third, who only got into the race because another horse was scratched at the gate at the last minute.

And the favorites? Out of the top three, only one made the top three of the actual race. The predicted winner came second. The others finished seventh or worse.

The trainers of the favorites all made the same mistake: they didn't ask what could go wrong. The pace was fast. They got sucked in. And when the final stretch came, their horses didn't have enough left.

This is what the rest of 2026 looks like.

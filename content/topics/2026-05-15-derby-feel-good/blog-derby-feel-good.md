I want to say something I believe completely, something this Derby confirmed for me, and I want you to sit with it before the weekend.

This is the year you surprise yourself.

I am the Firepig, and in over 35 years of readings, I have watched people discover capacities they did not know they had. It happens in every year. But the Fire Horse year makes it happen in a particular way: fast, dramatic, and often through a door that opens from someone else's misfortune.

That is not a threat. That is an invitation. And what I want you to understand before Monday is that you are already further along the path of readiness than you probably think.

## What Readiness Actually Looks Like

At the 2026 Derby, a horse named Ocelli was not supposed to be in the race. By the field rankings, he did not qualify for one of the top eighteen spots. In the final minutes before the horses entered the gate, another horse became agitated and had to be scratched. The slot opened. Ocelli stepped in.

Ocelli came third. At 70-to-1.

His jockey, Ashley Durr, got the mount because of that scratch. She rides full-time. She is a working professional who shows up every morning whether or not the day holds a historic race. When the call came, she did not need time to prepare. She was already prepared. The opportunity arrived from someone else's misfortune. The readiness was entirely her own.

And Ocelli: his team had kept him in condition. The trainer had kept him ready to run. Not for this specific race, because nobody could have known this slot would open. But the preparation was done. When the door swung open, there was no question of whether the horse and rider could step through. They just stepped through.

That is the readiness I am talking about.

It is not certainty. Ashley Durr did not know Friday morning that she would be riding in the Kentucky Derby on Saturday. Readiness is the condition of having done the preparation so that when the gun goes off, the question of whether you are ready is already answered. All that is left is to run.

## The Difference Between Waiting and Readiness

There is a version of patience that is actually avoidance. Waiting for the right moment that never quite arrives. Watching the race from the infield instead of the gate. Circling around a decision without committing. Staying comfortable because the situation has not yet forced your hand.

That is not what won the Derby. And it is not what the Fire Horse year rewards.

Golden Tempo was patient. He held back through the first three-quarters of the race. But he was not avoiding. His reserves were full because they were being saved for the moment, not because he had not committed. The moment the opening appeared, he was already in motion. The patience was in service of action, not instead of it.

The Fire Horse year is not a year of waiting. It is a year of readiness. The distinction matters. Waiting is passive. Readiness is active. You are doing something specific: preparing for the moment when the gun goes off and you need to have an answer ready.

## You Are Already on the Path

The Derby was two weeks ago. The Fire Horse year has eight months left.

Here is what I want you to take into the weekend. Readiness is not a binary state. You do not flip from not-ready to ready in a single moment. You build it in pieces, over time, often without knowing which specific door the preparation is for.

Jose Ortiz rode six Derbies before he won one. Six Derbies. Every morning of every one of those years, he was building the readiness that finally met its moment in 2026. He did not know in year one that the payoff would come in year seven. He just kept doing the work.

You do not need seven years. You are not starting from zero. The preparation you have done, in whatever form, for whatever goal, is already in the account. The Fire Horse year creates conditions where extraordinary things happen to people who were ready for them. The year does not care whether you feel ready. It cares whether the preparation is done.

And most of the time, the preparation is more done than people realize. The doubt is not evidence of unreadiness. It is just doubt.

## The Weekend Challenge

Here is what I want you to do before Monday.

Write down one thing you have been almost ready for. Not planning indefinitely, not dreaming about some future version of yourself. Something you are actually close to ready for, where the remaining gap between where you are and ready is something you could close in the next thirty days.

Now write down what you would do if the door opened tomorrow. Not what you hope you would do. What you would actually do, based on what you have prepared so far.

If the answer is clear, you are closer to ready than you thought. That is good news. The preparation is already working.

If the answer is unclear, the preparation work is in front of you. And the year is still early enough to do it.

Readiness is all this year. When the gun goes off, each of us has to take action. That is what a Fire Horse year is. And this year belongs to the people who show up having done the work, even if they are not certain the door will open.

Be the team that kept the horse ready and the jockey in the saddle. Not because you knew the call was coming. Because you prepared as if it might.

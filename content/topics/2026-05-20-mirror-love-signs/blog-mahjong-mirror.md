Two tiles keep appearing in love readings this year.

The Peach Blossom. The Sword.

Neither of them is comfortable to see on the table. Neither of them is bad news in the way most people assume. Both are clarifying. Both are pointing at the same essential thing: something is opposing the love life you want, and the Mirror is asking you to look at it directly.

I'm the Firepig, and in over 35 years of readings, I've watched people resist the Third Angle more than any other. It makes sense. The Third Angle of the Mahjong Mirror asks: what is opposing you? Not what you're fighting about, not who's to blame, but what is actually in the way. That question requires you to be honest about something you may have been working very hard not to see.

The Fire Horse year doesn't let that work go on forever.

## The Third Angle: What's Blocking You?

The Mahjong Mirror works through four angles of reflection. Most people rush to the First Angle (your central theme) and the Fourth Angle (your future direction). Those angles are comfortable because they feel productive. You're building something, you're moving toward something.

The Third Angle slows everything down. It asks a harder question.

In a love reading, the Third Angle isn't about your partner's flaws. It isn't about the argument you had last week or the habit they can't break. It's about the structural obstacle, the thing that would still be there even if the argument stopped and the habit changed.

Those structural obstacles are where the tiles become precise.

## What the Peach Blossom Card Is Telling You

The Peach Blossom is the card of romantic temptation. Affairs. The pull of someone new.

When I say "temptation" here, I don't only mean the obvious interpretation: that there's someone else in the picture. I mean the full range of what the Peach Blossom points to. Sometimes it's literal: an attraction outside the relationship that hasn't been acted on but is taking up emotional space. Sometimes it means the hunger for romance itself, the desire to feel pursued and wanted and seen in the way a new connection makes you feel, has become stronger than the relationship is currently feeding.

The Peach Blossom appearing in the opposition position isn't a verdict. It's a question. It asks: what need is this representing? What is your current relationship not providing that is making outside attention feel like an answer?

That is a Third Angle question. And the Fire Horse year amplifies the Peach Blossom's pull significantly.

In the years I've been reading, I have almost never seen someone act on a Peach Blossom temptation and find that it solved the problem the card was pointing to. What the Peach Blossom shows is an unmet need. Acting on the temptation distracts from the need rather than addressing it. The structural obstacle remains. Often, it gets more complicated.

The useful response to the Peach Blossom in opposition is to name the unmet need clearly and then decide whether your current relationship is the place where that need can be met. That is an honest and sometimes difficult conversation. It is also the conversation that actually moves something.

## What the Sword Card Is Telling You

The Sword is different. Where the Peach Blossom points to what's pulling you somewhere else, the Sword points to what needs to end or be cut cleanly.

I want to be careful here because the Sword gets misread. Seeing the Sword in a reading does not automatically mean "this relationship is over." The Sword can point to a pattern within a relationship that needs to end: a way of fighting that's become habitual and corrosive, a dynamic of emotional withdrawal that's been running for years, a story you've been telling yourself about your partner that stopped being true a long time ago.

The Sword says: something has been allowed to continue that needs to stop. The question is what.

When the Sword appears in the opposition position of a love reading, I ask a specific question: what is the thing in this relationship that both people know needs to change, and neither of them has been willing to name directly?

Almost always, there's an answer. People know. They came to the reading knowing. The Sword just gives them permission to say it.

The Fire Horse year has been producing more Sword readings in love positions than I've seen in a long time. That matches the energy: the Horse doesn't tolerate things that aren't working. It accelerates the end of what was already ending. If you've been feeling that acceleration, the Sword is likely part of the picture.

## How the Third Angle Works With What Monday Showed You

If you read Monday's post, you found your sign's headline and your one action. The Third Angle deepens that. Your sign's pattern (the Dragon's drift toward career, the Rabbit's instinct to avoid the fast pace, the Sheep's rare opening) is the surface layer. The Third Angle asks what's beneath it.

For example: the Dragon in a career-focus year isn't just a workaholic. There's usually a structural opposition underneath: the Dragon's need for achievement is competing with the relationship's need for presence, and no one has said that out loud. The Peach Blossom appearing in a Dragon reading this year doesn't mean they're pursuing someone else. It often means their partner is. Because the Dragon has been absent in all the ways that matter.

That's the Third Angle. Not the surface pattern, but the structural truth beneath it.

## What to Do Before the Year Shows You

The Third Angle isn't comfortable to work with. It's designed not to be. But there's a real advantage to doing this work yourself rather than waiting for the year to surface it for you.

When you identify your structural opposition clearly, you have the choice about how to respond. You can address it directly. You can decide it's not as threatening as it appeared. You can have the conversation, make the cut, or choose the stay with full clarity about what you're staying for.

When the year surfaces it for you (and in a Fire Horse year, it will), you don't get that choice. The revelation is harder, the timing is worse, and the emotional cost is higher.

Three questions to work with this week:

**What is the unmet need** in my current relationship (or in what I'm looking for in a relationship) that I haven't said out loud?

**What pattern** in my love life keeps repeating, no matter who the other person is?

**What would I cut** if I were being fully honest about what's no longer serving me?

Those are Third Angle questions. They are harder than the First Angle questions. They are also the ones that produce the most lasting change.

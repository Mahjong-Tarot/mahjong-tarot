# Mahjong Tarot — Channels & Guidelines

## Project

- **Website:** mahjongtarot.com
- **Author:** Bill Hajdu — mahjong practitioner and teacher, author of *The Mahjong Mirror*, combines Eastern mahjong tradition with Western tarot practice. Based in Seattle.

## Target Audience

Middle-aged women interested in astrology, tarot, mahjong, and self-reflection. Curious, spiritual, and smart.

## Brand Voice

**"Confidence and stillness"** — editorial, mystical but grounded, ancient game meets modern introspection.

**Tone:** Warm, reflective, mystical but accessible. Like a wise friend reading your cards across the table. Never preachy, never new-age cliche.

## Channel Guidelines

### Blog

- **Length:** 800–1,500 words.
- Every post connects to mahjong symbolism, tarot readings, or astrological cycles.
- Open with a scene or question that draws the reader in.
- Use subheadings that guide the journey.
- Write for women who are curious, spiritual, and smart.
- No jargon without explaining it.

### Facebook

- **Length:** 2–4 sentences.
- Warm, community-oriented.
- Works in astrology/tarot/mahjong groups.
- Mystical but relatable.
- Always end with: **[Link in first comment]**

### Instagram

- Standalone content. Visual-first.
- Card of the day, tile readings, reflective questions, behind the book.
- **Not repurposed from blog.**
- Caption should feel like a journal entry.

### LinkedIn

- Professional angle — how ancient wisdom applies to modern decision-making, career crossroads, leadership intuition.
- Staccato lines.
- 3–5 hashtags.

### X

- 280 characters max.
- A single mystical insight, surprising connection, or the question the cards are asking today.

## Image Prompt Guidelines

When generating image prompts for this project, push for maximum creative variety:

- **No repetitive motifs.** If snakes appear in one prompt, they don't need to appear in five more. One snake image is enough. Same for any single symbol - tiles, flames, constellations.
- **Vary the medium.** Mix across bold typography, cartoon/illustration, photorealistic still life, cinematic silhouette, infographic, pop art, painterly, newspaper collage, nature-as-metaphor. Don't cluster around one style.
- **Vary the subject.** Not every image needs astrology symbols. Some should be purely emotional or conceptual - weather, shadows, objects, scenes that carry the idea without literal zodiac references.
- **When a concept has tension (danger vs. opportunity, risk vs. reward), explore multiple visual metaphors for that tension.** Don't settle on one split-screen treatment. Try: cracked vs. whole objects, storm vs. calm, tabloid vs. prestige, fire vs. water, destruction vs. construction.
- **Stretch beyond the obvious.** The first idea that comes to mind is the one every AI image tool would generate. Go past it. The best prompts make the viewer feel something before they understand the article's topic.

Out of the top three favorites at the Derby, only one made the top three of the actual race. The horse predicted to win came second. The others didn't crack the top six.

This wasn't bad luck. It was a strategy problem.

The pace was set faster than most Derbies. The favorites' trainers did not ask: what happens to our horse if the pace runs fast from the start? They assumed their talent was enough. They got sucked into the crowd's pace. And by the final stretch, they were out of horse.

Meanwhile, Golden Tempo sat at the back, conserving everything. His trainer had thought it through. What's the obstacle? What can beat us? A fast pace, run at from the front, burns the reserve. So we don't run from the front. We wait.

In business, in relationships, in any decision you're making this year: if you're betting on the obvious play because everyone else is backing it, read the Monday blog post first. The Fire Horse year has a way of making favorites look foolish and long shots look wise.
